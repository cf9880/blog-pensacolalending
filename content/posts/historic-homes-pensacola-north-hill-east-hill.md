**Buying a historic home in Pensacola usually means shopping in one of three city historic districts: the Pensacola (Seville) Historic District, the North Hill Preservation District, or the Old East Hill Preservation District. These homes offer character and walkability, but exterior changes need city Architectural Review Board approval, and older systems can affect insurance and financing.**

Pensacola calls itself America's first settlement, and its neighborhoods show it. If a Queen Anne porch or a 1920s bungalow on a brick street is on your wish list, here's what to know before you make an offer.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three residential historic districts** are under the city's Architectural Review Board (ARB): Pensacola Historic District, North Hill, and Old East Hill.
- **Exterior work needs approval.** The ARB reviews exterior renovations and new construction, and it meets on the third Thursday of each month.
- **Insurance comes first.** Older roofs, wiring, and plumbing can affect insurability and premiums, so get quotes early.
- **Financing options include** conventional, FHA, VA, and renovation loans that can roll repair costs into one mortgage.
- **Market context:** The Pensacola Association of REALTORS® reported a $319,900 median sale price for September 2026.

## Which Pensacola Neighborhoods Are Historic Districts?

According to the City of Pensacola's Preservation District Design Guidelines, the city's historic districts include the Pensacola, North Hill, and Old East Hill historic districts. The Palafox Historic Business District covers the downtown commercial core.

### Pensacola Historic District (Seville)

This was the first historic district designated in the city and, the guidelines note, one of the oldest in Florida. Its boundaries were formalized in 1968, and it was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1970. The area grew over about 200 years, through the British period (1763-1781), the second Spanish period (1781-1821), and the 19th-century port and lumber era. You'll find frame vernacular homes and Gulf Coast or Creole cottages, plus Historic Pensacola Village and Seville Square.

### North Hill Preservation District

North Hill is roughly bounded by Blount, DeVilliers, Wright, and Palafox Streets. It developed in the 1870s as a residential suburb for Pensacola's upper-middle class. The city's guidelines say grand Queen Anne and Classical Revival homes went up at a "feverish rate" between 1870 and 1910, followed by bungalows and revival styles into the 1920s. Residents near Seville Square even nicknamed it "Snob Hill." North Hill was locally designated in 1974 and listed on the National Register in 1983.

### Old East Hill Preservation District

Old East Hill is bounded roughly by Gadsden Street, 9th Avenue, Wright Street, and Hayne Street. Most homes date from after 1870 and include frame vernacular, folk Victorian, modest Queen Anne cottages, and bungalows. Residents formed the East Hill Preservation Association and drafted its preservation zoning in 1984. It's the smallest of the city's historic districts.

## What Does the Architectural Review Board Approve?

The [City of Pensacola Architectural Review Board](https://cityofpensacola.com/164/Architectural-Review-Board) approves or denies plans for buildings to be built, renovated, or demolished in the historic and preservation districts. In practice, that can include:

- Replacing windows, doors, roofing, or siding
- Adding a porch, addition, fence, or garage
- Changing paint colors (some minor items qualify for an abbreviated review)

The ARB meets monthly on the third Thursday at 2 p.m. at City Hall. The city's 2026 schedule lists application deadlines 21 days before each meeting. If you plan exterior work after closing, build that timing into your plans.

**Tax tip:** The city's guidelines note that in 1994, the City of Pensacola and Escambia County adopted an ad valorem tax exemption for restoring historic properties. Ask the city or county about current eligibility before you count on it.

## How Do Insurance and Inspections Work on Older Homes?

Insurance is often the biggest surprise with historic homes in Florida. Before you're under contract:

1. **Get insurance quotes early.** Roof age and the condition of electrical, plumbing, and heating systems can affect whether a policy is available and what it costs. Many Florida insurers ask for a four-point inspection on older homes.
2. **Order a wind mitigation inspection.** Features like roof-to-wall connections and opening protection may lower your premium. Our [Pensacola wind mitigation guide](/posts/pensacola-home-insurance-wind-mitigation/) explains how it works.
3. **Check flood zones.** Parts of the Seville district sit close to Pensacola Bay. Look up the FEMA flood map before you write an offer.
4. **Hire an inspector who knows older homes.** Ask about wood rot, foundation piers, termites, and older wiring.

## Which Loans Work for Historic Homes in Pensacola?

- **Conventional loans:** Qualified buyers may put as little as 3% to 5% down, up to the 2026 conforming limit of $832,750 for a one-unit home.
- **FHA loans:** 3.5% down with a 580+ credit score. FHA appraisals have property condition standards, so major repairs may need to be fixed first. See our [FHA loan guide for Pensacola](/posts/fha-loan-pensacola-fl/).
- **VA loans:** Eligible veterans and service members can often buy with no down payment. The home must meet VA minimum property requirements.
- **Renovation loans:** Programs such as FHA 203(k) and conventional renovation loans can combine the purchase price and repair costs into one mortgage, based on the home's after-repair value.
- **HELOCs:** Already own a historic home? A home equity line of credit can help fund approved projects over time.

Rates matter, too. Mortgage News Daily put the average 30-year fixed at 7.18% on November 9, 2026, and Freddie Mac's weekly survey showed 7.18% on November 5, down for a third straight week.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the historic districts in Pensacola?

The City of Pensacola's residential historic districts are the Pensacola (Seville) Historic District, the North Hill Preservation District, and the Old East Hill Preservation District. The Palafox Historic Business District covers downtown.

### Do I need approval to renovate a historic home in Pensacola?

Usually, for exterior work. The city's Architectural Review Board reviews exterior renovations, new construction, and demolition in the historic districts. Some minor items, like paint color changes, can use an abbreviated review.

### Can I get an FHA loan on a historic home?

Yes, if the home meets FHA property standards. Major repairs may need to be completed before closing, or you can look at a renovation loan such as an FHA 203(k).

### Is it harder to insure an older home in Florida?

It can be. Roof age and the condition of electrical, plumbing, and heating systems affect coverage and cost, and many insurers ask for a four-point inspection. Get quotes before your inspection period ends.

### When was North Hill built?

North Hill developed mainly from the 1870s through the 1920s. The city's guidelines say many Queen Anne and Classical Revival homes were built between 1870 and 1910.
