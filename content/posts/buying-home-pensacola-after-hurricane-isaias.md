**Yes, you can still buy a home in Pensacola after Hurricane Isaias, but plan for a few extra steps.** Expect a re-inspection, an updated appraisal or property condition check, and questions from insurers before they'll write a new policy. With the right team and a little extra time, buyers are still closing on homes across Escambia and Santa Rosa counties.

Our thoughts are with every neighbor still cleaning up. Here's a calm, practical guide for anyone buying or selling a home this fall.

## Key takeaways

- **Isaias made landfall near Destin on October 9, 2026,** as a Category 2 hurricane, knocking out power to nearly 450,000 customers in Northwest Florida (WUWF).
- **Insurance comes first.** Get quotes before you make an offer, and expect insurers to ask about storm damage.
- **Re-inspect everything:** roof, trees, fences, electrical service, and water intrusion.
- **Lenders may require** an updated appraisal or condition report for homes in the affected area.
- **Plan for delays** and choose a rate lock that leaves a cushion.

## What happened during Hurricane Isaias?

According to WUWF's recovery coverage, Isaias toppled trees and power lines, damaged homes and businesses, and disrupted water service across the region. Escambia County estimated about **$64 million** in damage to public facilities and recovery costs, not counting private property. Santa Rosa County classified more than 80 structures as destroyed and about 300 with major damage in its early estimates.

As of October 20, 2026, Florida had requested a federal Major Disaster Declaration that includes Escambia and Santa Rosa. FEMA individual assistance was not yet open. Local officials are asking residents to **report damage to their county** now, because that data supports the request.

## How does a hurricane affect a home purchase under contract?

Most Florida purchase contracts address damage that happens before closing. Typical outcomes:

1. **The seller repairs** the damage before closing.
2. **The price or credits change** to cover repairs.
3. **The buyer may be able to cancel** if damage is significant, depending on the contract.

Your agent and a real estate attorney can explain your specific contract. On the loan side, your lender will want to know the property is still in acceptable condition. That might mean an **updated appraisal**, a **re-inspection**, or photos showing completed repairs.

**Binding restriction (definition):** a temporary pause when insurers won't write new policies in an area threatened by a storm. Many carriers lift these soon after a storm passes, but some require a signed no-damage statement or an inspection first. Each insurer has its own rules.

## What should Pensacola buyers inspect after a hurricane?

Ask your inspector to look closely at:

- **Roof:** missing shingles, lifted edges, damaged flashing, and roof age. Roof condition is a big factor for Florida insurers.
- **Trees:** cracked limbs, leaning trunks, or roots lifted by wind.
- **Water intrusion:** stains, soft drywall, musty smells, or moisture in the attic.
- **Electrical service:** damaged meters or service masts may need an electrician before power is restored safely.
- **Fences, sheds, and docks:** these are easy to overlook and can be costly.

**Tip:** ask the seller for storm repair invoices, permits, and any insurance claim records.

## How do I handle homeowners and flood insurance in Pensacola?

**Start shopping for insurance the day you start shopping for homes.** It's one of the biggest monthly costs here.

- **Citizens Property Insurance** publishes average rate changes by county. Its 2026 filing showed an average Escambia County premium of about **$3,244**, down 3.8%. Your quote could be very different.
- **Wind mitigation inspection:** documents features like roof straps, impact windows, and roof deck nailing. These can qualify for insurance discounts.
- **My Safe Florida Home:** after you homestead the home, you may qualify for a **free wind mitigation inspection** and **matching grants up to $10,000** for upgrades like impact windows or roof-to-wall straps. Grant homes must be built before 2008 and insured for $700,000 or less, and you must be approved before work begins. ([My Safe Florida Home](https://mysafeflhome.com/faqs-2/))
- **Flood insurance:** required by your lender in FEMA high-risk zones, and smart to price everywhere. Standard homeowners policies do not cover flooding, and National Flood Insurance Program policies typically have a **30-day waiting period**. ([FloodSmart.gov](https://www.floodsmart.gov/))

**Escrow (definition):** an account your lender uses to pay property taxes and insurance for you. Your monthly payment includes these amounts, so insurance costs directly affect what you can afford.
