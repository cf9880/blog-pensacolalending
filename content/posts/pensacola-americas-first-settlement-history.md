**Pensacola is often called "America's First Settlement" because Spanish explorer Tristán de Luna y Arellano founded a colony on Pensacola Bay in 1559. The University of West Florida identifies it as the earliest multi-year European settlement ever archaeologically identified in the United States, predating St. Augustine by six years and Jamestown by 48 years.**

With Thanksgiving next week and family in town, this is a good time to show visitors (or yourself, if you are thinking about moving here) just how deep Pensacola history runs. Here is the short version of the story, where to see it, and what living in a city this old means for home buyers.

## Key Takeaways

- **1559:** Luna's fleet of 11 ships arrived on Pensacola Bay with about 1,500 people, according to UWF.
- **A September 1559 hurricane** wrecked most of the fleet, and the colony was withdrawn by August 1561.
- **2015:** UWF archaeologists identified the settlement site in a developed Pensacola neighborhood.
- **"City of Five Flags":** five governments have ruled Pensacola over the centuries.
- **For buyers:** older homes and Gulf weather mean inspections, insurance and flood checks matter as much as the rate.

## What Happened at the 1559 Luna Settlement?

According to UWF historian and archaeologist [Dr. John Worth's Luna chronology](https://pages.uwf.edu/jworth/jw_Spanfla_Luna.html), Luna's fleet left Veracruz, Mexico, on June 11, 1559, with some 1,500 soldiers and colonists and 240 horses. The ships sailed into Pensacola Bay on August 14, 1559, and the settlers named their town Santa María de Ochuse.

Then the Gulf did what the Gulf sometimes does. On September 19, 1559, a hurricane struck the ships at anchor, leaving only three afloat. The colonists spent the next two years trying to survive, with relief fleets from New Spain and a temporary move inland. By August 1561 the last of them had withdrawn.

## How Was the Settlement Rediscovered?

For centuries no one knew exactly where Luna's town stood. In October 2015, Pensacola native Tom Garner found Spanish colonial and Native American artifacts on a private residential lot within view of two Luna shipwrecks in the bay. [UWF announced the discovery](https://news.uwf.edu/luna/) on December 17, 2015, after test excavations turned up 16th-century Spanish ceramics, olive jar pieces and trade beads.

To protect the neighborhood and the site, UWF has not disclosed the exact location. That means the oldest European settlement site in the country sits quietly among ordinary Pensacola homes.

## Why Is Pensacola Called the City of Five Flags?

[Visit Pensacola](https://www.visitpensacola.com/things-to-do/history-heritage/multicultural/) explains that the nickname comes from the five governments that have ruled the city. Highlights include:

- **Spain**, beginning with Luna in 1559 and returning through several periods.
- **France**, which captured Pensacola in 1719 and was driven out by a hurricane in 1722.
- **Great Britain**, which arrived in 1763, made Pensacola the capital of British West Florida and laid out the downtown streets around Seville Square.
- **Spain again**, after the 1781 Battle of Pensacola.
- **The United States**, with the formal transfer at Plaza Ferdinand VII in July 1821, plus a period under the **Confederacy** during the Civil War.

Visit Pensacola also notes that the city is named for the Panzacola people, who lived here for centuries before European colonization.

## Where Can You See Pensacola History This Holiday Season?

These spots make an easy outing when relatives are in town:

- **Pensacola Museum of History.** Housed in the 1907 former City Hall, it features the "City of Five Flags" exhibit, and Visit Pensacola says Luna settlement artifacts can be seen there.
- **Historic Pensacola** (330 S. Jefferson St.), with historic homes and museums downtown.
- **Plaza Ferdinand VII**, where Florida was formally transferred to the United States in 1821.
- **Seville Square** and the downtown street grid the British laid out.
- **Plaza de Luna** (900 S. Palafox St.), on the waterfront.
- **Pensacola Maritime Heritage Trail** along Bayfront Parkway.

Check each site's hours around Thanksgiving before you go. For more seasonal ideas, see our [Pensacola holiday events guide](/posts/pensacola-holiday-events-2026/).

## What Does Pensacola's History Mean for Home Buyers?

Living in an old city is part of the charm, but it shapes the buying process.

### Older homes need careful inspections

Neighborhoods near downtown have homes that are decades or more than a century old. Plan for a thorough inspection and ask about roofs, wiring, plumbing and foundations. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is Pensacola called America's first settlement?

Tristán de Luna's Spanish expedition settled on Pensacola Bay from 1559 to 1561. According to the University of West Florida, that is the earliest multi-year European colonial settlement ever archaeologically identified in the United States, six years before St. Augustine and 48 years before Jamestown.

### Where can I see Luna settlement artifacts in Pensacola?

Visit Pensacola says artifacts and information about the Luna site can be seen at the Pensacola Museum of History downtown. UWF's archaeology program also shares research through its public exhibits.

### Why is Pensacola called the City of Five Flags?

Because five governments have ruled the city over its history. Spain, France and Great Britain all held Pensacola before it became part of the United States in 1821, and the city was also under the Confederacy during the Civil War.
