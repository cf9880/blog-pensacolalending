## Key Takeaways

- **Florida law requires insurers to offer wind mitigation discounts** or credits for qualifying construction features (section 627.0629, Florida Statutes).
- **A new inspection form is in use.** The Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form OIR-B1-1802 (Rev. 04/26) is required for inspections on or after April 1, 2026, according to the Florida Department of Financial Services.
- **Reports last up to five years** as long as the home isn't materially changed and the form has no inaccuracies.
- **My Safe Florida Home** has offered free inspections and grants of up to $10,000 for eligible homesteaded homes. Check current funding before you plan around it.
- **Lower insurance can mean a lower payment** and can help your debt-to-income ratio when you apply for a loan.

## What Is a Wind Mitigation Inspection in Pensacola?

A wind mitigation inspection is a short visit, often under an hour, by a qualified inspector such as a licensed home inspector, general contractor, or engineer. The inspector records the features that help a home resist hurricane winds and completes the state form your insurer uses to apply discounts.

The Florida Department of Financial Services says the 2026 form is "more comprehensive with additional categories" and spells out the documentation needed to verify each entry. Inspections done before April 1, 2026, could use the older form, and those reports can remain valid for up to five years.

## What Does the Inspector Check?

The form covers features like these:

- **Building code:** Whether the home was built to the Florida Building Code.
- **Roof covering:** The age and permit history of the roof.
- **Roof deck attachment:** How the roof decking is nailed down.
- **Roof-to-wall attachment:** Toe nails, clips, single wraps, or double wraps.
- **Roof shape:** Hip roofs generally perform better in wind than gable roofs.
- **Secondary water resistance:** A sealed layer under the shingles.
- **Opening protection:** Impact-rated windows, doors, and garage doors, or approved shutters.

Your savings depend on your insurer's filed rates, so two homes with the same features can see different discounts with different companies. Ask your agent to show the credits on your declarations page.

## How Can My Safe Florida Home Help?

[My Safe Florida Home](https://mysafeflhome.com/), run by the Florida Department of Financial Services, has offered:

- **Free wind mitigation inspections** for eligible homeowners
- **Grants up to $10,000** for upgrades such as roof-to-wall connections, impact windows and doors, and garage doors
- **Matching grants** of $2 for every $1 the homeowner contributes, with low-income applicants eligible for grants without a match

Eligibility rules have included owning a **homesteaded** single-family home or townhome with an insured value of **$700,000 or less**. You must have your grant approved **before** work begins. Funding has come in waves, and the Miami Herald reported this year that lawmakers did not add new money while about $440 million remained unspent. Check the program's website or call 850-427-2559 for the current status before you count on a grant.

If you're buying, note that many benefits require a homestead exemption, which you can apply for after you own and live in the home.

## How Does Home Insurance Affect Your Pensacola Mortgage?

Your lender includes homeowners insurance (and flood insurance, if required) in your monthly payment. A higher premium can:

- **Raise your monthly payment** through your escrow account
- **Raise your debt-to-income ratio,** which can lower how much home you qualify for
- **Cause an escrow shortage** at renewal if premiums jump mid-year

Here's an illustrative example. If a wind mitigation report cuts a $4,200 annual premium to $3,400, your escrow payment drops by about $67 per month. On a loan, that can make a real difference in what you qualify for. These numbers are examples only, not a quote or a promised discount.

## Should Buyers Get a Wind Mitigation Report Before Closing?

Usually, yes. Ask whether the seller has a current report, and if not, schedule one along with your home inspection and four-point inspection if the home is older. Share it with your insurance agent early so your quote reflects the home's features. That helps your lender close on time and avoids surprises in your final numbers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much can a wind mitigation inspection save in Pensacola?

It depends on your home's features and your insurer's filed rates. Florida law requires insurers to offer discounts or credits for qualifying features like hip roofs, roof-to-wall clips or straps, and impact-rated openings. Ask your agent to show the credits on your policy.

### How long is a Florida wind mitigation report valid?

A completed Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form is valid for up to five years, as long as the home has no material changes and the form has no inaccuracies, according to the Florida Department of Financial Services.

### What changed with the wind mitigation form in 2026?

Florida's revised form, OIR-B1-1802 (Rev. 04/26), must be used for all inspections on or after April 1, 2026. It adds categories and specifies the documentation needed to verify each feature.

### Is My Safe Florida Home still giving grants?

The program has offered free inspections and grants up to $10,000 for eligible homesteaded homes, but funding comes and goes. Check mysafeflhome.com or call 850-427-2559 for the current status before you plan around a grant.

### Does homeowners insurance affect how much house I can afford?
